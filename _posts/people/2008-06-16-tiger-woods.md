## Summary

Tiger Woods' victory at the 2008 US Open on June 16 stands as one of professional sports' most remarkable achievements — a major championship victory accomplished while the defending champion played on a torn anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) and a stress fracture of the tibia in his left knee. Woods defeated American golfer Rocco Mediate in a playoff at Torrey Pines Golf Club in San Diego — level after an 18-hole Monday round and settled on the first hole of sudden death — for his record 14th major championship victory, moving him closer to Jack Nicklaus's all-time record of 18.

The injury and comeback defined 2008 in golf and raised Woods to legendary status. His willingness to compete and win under such severe physical duress — and to delay surgery until after the tournament — exemplified the intensity and determination that had made him the sport's transcendent figure for nearly a decade.

## Career Context and Dominance

Born on December 30, 1975, Eldrick "Tiger" Tont Woods is the son of Earl Woods, a retired Army officer and accomplished golfer, and Kultida Punsawad, a Thai national. Woods demonstrated golfing prodigies from childhood, appearing on television programs as a young child and winning youth tournaments at remarkable rates. He turned professional in 1996 and won the 1997 Masters at age 21 by a record 12 strokes, immediately establishing himself as a generational talent.

By 2008, Woods had won 13 major championships — already the most in the modern era — and held the top world ranking for nearly a decade. His dominance was manifest across multiple dimensions: technical excellence, consistency, mental toughness, and physical athleticism that transcended typical stereotypes of professional golfers. He was the global face of golf and one of the world's highest-earning athletes.

## The 2008 US Open and Injury

The 108th United States Open Championship began on June 12 at Torrey Pines Golf Club (South Course) in La Jolla, California — a public course unlike most US Open venues, par 71, measuring 7,643 yards. The USGA set up the course at its traditional severe difficulty, with narrow fairways, high rough, and fast greens.

During the first round on June 12, Woods felt acute pain in his left knee and leg upon striking his drive on an early hole. Medical consultation during the tournament revealed a severe injury: a torn anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) in the left knee, complicated by a double stress fracture of the left tibia (shin bone) — injuries later confirmed to have been developing for months prior. The ACL tear alone typically requires surgery and 6–9 months of rehabilitation before competitive return. Woods had reportedly played the final weeks of preparation for the US Open while already nursing the stress fracture, limiting his practice to reduce impact on the bone.

Despite the severity, Woods continued and performed brilliantly across all four regulation rounds. He shot rounds of 72-68-70-73 (283 total, 1-under par on the 71-par South Course over 72 holes), finishing tied with Rocco Mediate at 1-under-par after 72 holes. Mediate, a 45-year-old American professional ranked 158th in the world at the time, had played the tournament of his career to keep pace with the sport's dominant figure. The contrast — Woods, limping visibly down fairways on a structurally compromised knee, and the unheralded Mediate competing fearlessly — gave the championship an almost cinematic quality that drew record television audiences.

Under US Open rules, ties after 72 holes are broken by an 18-hole playoff the following day. On June 16, 2008, Woods and Mediate returned to Torrey Pines for the playoff. The match was gripping: Mediate led late in the round before Woods birdied the 18th hole to force extra play, and the 18-hole playoff itself finished level, each man at even par. A sudden-death hole followed — the 91st of the championship, played at Torrey Pines' 7th — on which Mediate missed a par putt and Woods made par to win. The performance was hailed immediately and subsequently as one of the most extraordinary in the history of major championship golf.

## Medical Decision and Aftermath

Immediately following the US Open victory, Woods withdrew from competition to undergo ACL reconstruction surgery. The operation was performed on June 24, 2008, reconstructing the torn ligament with a tendon graft from his right thigh and repairing further cartilage damage. Rehabilitation was prolonged and challenging; Woods did not return to professional competition for roughly eight months, making his comeback at the WGC-Accenture Match Play Championship in late February 2009, where he lost in the second round to Tim Clark.

The injury and its management altered the trajectory of Woods' career. He returned to competition in 2009 but missed nearly the entire 2010 season while recovering. In 2011–2012, his personal life was shattered by scandal following an automobile accident and revelations of extramarital infidelities, leading to a painful divorce from his wife of six years, Elin Nordegren. His game suffered; he did not win another major championship from 2008 to 2018 — a 10-year drought that prompted debates about whether he would ever again reach the heights of his early dominance.

## Significance

The 2008 US Open victory and the manner in which it was achieved elevated Woods to near-mythic status:

**Sporting Courage**: Woods' decision to compete and win while seriously injured raised the standard for athletic perseverance and mental toughness. The performance was frequently cited as one of sport's greatest moments.

**Golf's Narrative**: The US Open victory, while not Woods' most dominant major championship win, became the most symbolically resonant for casual fans and cemented his status as the game's transcendent figure.

**Physical Toll and Longevity**: The injury and its long-term consequences illustrated the physical wear that elite professional golf can impose. Woods' subsequent struggles with back injuries (culminating in a 2017 spinal fusion) raised questions about sustainability of high-level competition into middle age.

**Later Redemption Arc**: After years of personal and competitive decline, Woods' 2019 Masters victory (his 15th major) at age 43 became a redemptive narrative — the comeback story completing the arc that began with the courageous 2008 US Open win.

### 2008: A Year of Superhuman Athletic Achievement

Woods' performance at Torrey Pines was one of three landmark individual athletic achievements that defined 2008 as a year of exceptional sport. At the [Beijing Summer Olympics]({{ '/news/history-politics/beijing-summer-olympics/' | relative_url }}) in August, swimmer [Michael Phelps]({{ '/news/people/michael-phelps/' | relative_url }}) won eight gold medals in a single Games — surpassing the previous record of seven — while sprinter [Usain Bolt]({{ '/news/people/usain-bolt/' | relative_url }}) set three world records in the sprint events. On the tennis courts, [Rafael Nadal]({{ '/news/people/rafael-nadal/' | relative_url }}) completed a historic sweep of the French Open, Wimbledon (in a five-set final widely acclaimed as the greatest match in history), and Olympic gold. Woods' US Open win on a torn ACL entered this same conversation: it was the performance against which individual athletic willpower in 2008 was measured.

The 2008 US Open exemplifies a moment when athletic excellence, physical courage, and human determination converged to create one of sport's most memorable achievements.
