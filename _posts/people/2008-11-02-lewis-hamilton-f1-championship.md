# Lewis Hamilton Wins 2008 Formula 1 World Championship

**Date:** November 2, 2008  
**Category:** People  
**Key figures:** Lewis Hamilton (McLaren), Felipe Massa (Ferrari), Ron Dennis (McLaren), Timo Glock (Toyota)

## Summary

Lewis Hamilton clinched his first Formula 1 World Championship on November 2, 2008, at the Brazilian Grand Prix in São Paulo — the final race of an 18-round season. Driving the McLaren MP4-23, the 23-year-old British driver won the title by a single point (98 to 97) over Ferrari's Felipe Massa in one of the most dramatic championship finishes in the sport's history.

With five laps remaining, Hamilton ran in sixth position and faced elimination from title contention. Massa was leading the race — his home Brazilian Grand Prix — and, had the finishing order held, would have taken the championship. On the final lap, in a rain-dampened final sector, Hamilton passed Toyota's Timo Glock, who had stayed out on dry-weather tyres as the track dampened during the closing laps. That single overtaking move lifted Hamilton from sixth to fifth, earning the one additional championship point he needed. Massa crossed the line first to win the race, and the Ferrari garage celebrated briefly before it became clear, moments later, that Hamilton had secured fifth place — and with it the title.

The final margin of one point was the smallest possible outcome in a season that had swung repeatedly between the two drivers across circuits from Australia to Brazil.

## The 2008 Season in Detail

The 2008 Formula One season ran from March 16 (Australian Grand Prix, Melbourne) to November 2 (Brazilian Grand Prix, São Paulo), comprising 18 races across five continents. Hamilton drove for the McLaren-Mercedes team, the same outfit with which he had debuted in 2007 — finishing second in the championship in his rookie year by a single point in nearly identical circumstances.

Key moments across the 2008 season:

- **March 16 — Australian GP (Melbourne):** Hamilton won from pole, an early statement of intent.
- **April 6 — Bahrain GP:** Massa won as Ferrari showed strong pace in the desert heat.
- **May 25 — Monaco GP:** Hamilton won a wet, chaotic race despite early tyre damage, moving into the championship lead.
- **June 8 — Canadian GP (Montréal):** Robert Kubica (BMW Sauber) took his only career win; Hamilton retired after colliding with Kimi Räikkönen in the pit lane while the exit light was red — a costly error.
- **July 6 — British GP (Silverstone):** Hamilton won in heavy rain by more than a minute, widely regarded as his finest drive of the year.
- **July 20 — German GP (Hockenheim):** Hamilton won after recovering from a mid-race safety-car setback.
- **August 3 — Hungarian GP:** Heikki Kovalainen (McLaren) took his only career win after Massa's engine failed late; Hamilton finished fifth with a deflating tyre.
- **September 14 — Italian GP (Monza):** Sebastian Vettel (Toro Rosso) won a wet race from pole, becoming the youngest Grand Prix winner at the time; Hamilton finished seventh.
- **October 12 — Japanese GP (Fuji):** Fernando Alonso (Renault) won; Hamilton took a first-corner penalty and finished twelfth, narrowing his points lead.
- **October 19 — Chinese GP (Shanghai):** Hamilton won commandingly, carrying a seven-point lead over Massa into the finale.
- **November 2 — Brazilian GP (São Paulo):** Massa won his home race, but Hamilton's fifth place on the final lap gave him the championship by a single point.

McLaren's MP4-23 was noted for its tyre management in wet conditions and consistent aerodynamic balance across varied track types. Ferrari's F2008 was faster in a straight line at several venues but suffered reliability problems early in the season that ultimately cost Massa.

## Key Figures

**Lewis Hamilton** — Born January 7, 1985, in Stevenage, Hertfordshire, England. Hamilton was in his second full F1 season. He had been scouted and mentored by McLaren from karting age — an unusually early professional relationship that shaped his development. His father, Anthony Hamilton, worked multiple jobs to fund early karting costs before McLaren assumed patronage. In 2008 Hamilton won five Grands Prix (Australia, Monaco, Great Britain, Germany, China).

**Felipe Massa** — Born April 25, 1981, in São Paulo, Brazil. Massa won six Grands Prix in 2008 (Bahrain, Turkey, France, the European GP at Valencia, Belgium, and his home Brazilian GP) — more race wins than Hamilton — but finished a single point behind in the championship, the result of retirements and non-scoring finishes in several earlier rounds. His victory in his home Brazilian race made the final-lap reversal especially poignant.

**Timo Glock** — Born March 18, 1982, in Lindenfels, Germany. Toyota's decision to keep Glock on dry-weather tyres as rain returned in the closing laps was a strategy gamble that backfired: he lost grip through the final sector on the last lap, allowing Hamilton to pass for fifth — the decisive championship position.

**Ron Dennis** — McLaren's long-serving team principal (1981–2009), who had guided Hamilton through the junior McLaren programme and oversaw the MP4-23 campaign.

**Kimi Räikkönen** — Reigning (2007) world champion and Massa's Ferrari teammate. He finished third in the 2008 Brazilian Grand Prix and third in the drivers' championship on 75 points, 23 behind Hamilton.

## Historical Significance

Hamilton's 2008 championship carried multiple layers of historical meaning:

### Youngest Champion (at the time)

At 23 years and 300 days, Hamilton became the youngest Formula 1 World Champion at the time — breaking the record previously held by Emerson Fittipaldi, who was 25 when he won in 1972. (The record was subsequently broken by Sebastian Vettel, who won at 23 years and 134 days in 2010.)

### First Driver of Black Heritage

Hamilton's paternal grandfather emigrated from Grenada, and his father, Anthony Hamilton, is of Grenadian descent, making Lewis the first driver of Black heritage to win the FIA Formula One World Championship. In a sport with roots in post-war European motorsport and limited diversity at elite levels, the breakthrough attracted global media attention. Hamilton later became a prominent advocate for racial equality in motorsport, founding the Hamilton Commission in 2020 and launching the Mission 44 charitable initiative.

### Cultural and Commercial Impact

The 2008 championship brought Formula 1 significantly wider cultural visibility in the United Kingdom and across the Caribbean diaspora. The championship-deciding Brazilian Grand Prix drew a large UK television audience on ITV. Hamilton's personal brand — articulate, media-trained from youth, and active in music and fashion circles — attracted demographic segments not previously engaged by the sport.

## Legacy and Later Career

Hamilton went on to win six further World Championships — 2014, 2015, 2017, 2018, 2019, and 2020, all with Mercedes-AMG Petronas — giving him seven titles and equalling Michael Schumacher's all-time record. He also holds the all-time Formula 1 record for career pole positions (104) and has won more than 100 Grands Prix. The 2008 season, however, remains the formative championship — won against the odds, decided in the final seconds, and carrying singular historical weight.
