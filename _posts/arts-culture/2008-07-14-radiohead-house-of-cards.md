## Summary

In July 2008, the English rock band Radiohead released the music video for "House of Cards," a track from their album *In Rainbows*. Directed by James Frost, the video was produced without any cameras or conventional lighting: performers and landscapes were recorded as three-dimensional data using two laser-scanning technologies, then rendered as shifting clouds of points. On 14 July 2008, Radiohead and its collaborators published the underlying capture data as open source under a Creative Commons license, hosting it on Google Code and inviting the public to create their own visualizations from the raw material. The project extended the band's reputation — established the previous year by *In Rainbows*' pay-what-you-want digital launch — for pairing musical releases with unconventional technology and distribution.

## A Video Filmed Without Cameras

The "House of Cards" video used no photographic capture at all. Instead, it combined two systems that record physical space as three-dimensional data. Close-range subjects — including the face of frontman Thom Yorke — were scanned with a structured-light system developed by Geometric Informatics, which projects patterns of light to reconstruct surfaces in fine detail. Wider environments, such as suburban streets and a party scene, were captured with a Velodyne LIDAR unit whose array of 64 lasers rotated a full 360 degrees roughly 900 times per minute, measuring distances to everything around it.

The resulting footage rendered people and places as grids and drifting constellations of illuminated points rather than solid images, giving the video a fragile, dissolving quality suited to the song's theme. Because the data described geometry rather than pixels, the imagery could be rotated, re-lit, and re-rendered after the fact, a flexibility impossible with ordinary film.

## Open-Source Data and a Participatory Release

Rather than treat the capture data as proprietary, Radiohead released it publicly. On 14 July 2008 the animation data were licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-Noncommercial-Share Alike 3.0 license and made available on Google Code, distributed as raw CSV point data alongside code written in the Processing programming environment. The public was invited to use the material freely, within the license terms, to build their own videos and interactive experiments; the accompanying announcement expressed anticipation about the uses people would find for it.

The release turned a finished music video into a shared toolkit, prompting a wave of fan-made reinterpretations and interactive online versions and making the project an early, widely cited example of open, participatory media in popular music. At the 51st Annual Grammy Awards in February 2009, the "House of Cards" video was nominated for Best Short Form Music Video, and the song received nominations for Best Rock Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal and Best Rock Song.

## Context: *In Rainbows* and Radiohead's Digital Experiments

"House of Cards" came from *In Rainbows*, Radiohead's seventh studio album and the record that had made the band a symbol of music-industry disruption. Self-released on 10 October 2007 as a pay-what-you-want download that let listeners set their own price, the album reached retail through XL Recordings internationally in December 2007 and through TBD Records in North America on 1 January 2008. The physical edition debuted at number one on both the UK Albums Chart and the US Billboard 200, demonstrating that the unconventional digital rollout had not undercut conventional sales. Through 2008 the band supported the album with an extensive international tour.

The laser-scanned, open-data "House of Cards" video was of a piece with that experimentation: where *In Rainbows* had challenged how music was priced and distributed, the video challenged how a music video was made and owned. It arrived in a year when digitally native distribution was reshaping other corners of the culture, from the [App Store]({{ '/news/science-technology/apple-app-store-launch/' | relative_url }}) that opened days earlier to the album releases of contemporaries such as [Coldplay]({{ '/news/arts-culture/coldplay-viva-la-vida/' | relative_url }}).

## Significance

The "House of Cards" video is remembered less for its chart impact than for its method. By replacing cameras with data capture and then giving that data away, Radiohead helped popularize the use of LIDAR and structured-light scanning as expressive artistic tools and offered an early template for open-source, remixable audiovisual work. The project reinforced the band's role — begun with *In Rainbows* — as a testing ground for how digital technology could change the creation, distribution, and ownership of music, and it remains a frequently referenced case study at the intersection of art, code, and data.
