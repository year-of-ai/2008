**Key figures**: Lil Wayne (Dwayne Michael Carter Jr., artist), Birdman (Bryan Williams, Cash Money Records founder and executive producer), Static Major (featured artist and co-writer of "Lollipop"), T-Pain (featured artist on "Got Money"), Jay-Z (featured artist on "Mr. Carter"), Kanye West (producer and featured artist on "Dr. Carter"), Jim Jonsin and Bangladesh (producers)

## Summary

Lil Wayne released *Tha Carter III* on June 10, 2008, via Cash Money Records and Universal Republic. The album became the best-selling album of 2008 in the United States, selling over 1 million copies in its first week and approximately 2.88 million copies by year-end according to Nielsen SoundScan — a figure that topped every other album across all genres, including rock, pop, and country. It cemented Lil Wayne's position as hip-hop's dominant commercial force and stands as a defining cultural artifact of 2008 popular music.

## Background and Build-Up

*Tha Carter III* was the third album in Lil Wayne's Carter series, following *Tha Carter* (2004) and *Tha Carter II* (2005), which had established him as a respected mixtape and album artist within hip-hop. Between 2006 and 2008, Lil Wayne released a prolific string of acclaimed mixtapes — including *Dedication 2* (2006), *Da Drought 3* (2007), and *Dedication 3* (2008) — that built extraordinary anticipation and positioned him as arguably the most talked-about rapper alive.

The album's release had been delayed multiple times, and segments leaked online throughout 2007, creating an unusual situation in which some of the strongest material circulated widely before the official release. Rather than damaging sales, the leaks intensified demand. Cash Money scheduled the release for June 10, 2008, supported by aggressive radio promotion of the lead single "Lollipop," released in March 2008.

A legal dimension surrounded the release: in July 2007, Lil Wayne had been arrested in New York City after a loaded handgun was found on his tour bus following a concert at the Beacon Theatre. The weapons charge was still pending when *Tha Carter III* was released; he would later plead guilty and serve roughly eight months at Rikers Island in 2010.

## Tracklist and Key Songs

*Tha Carter III* combined radio-oriented singles with dense lyrical showcases. Key tracks included:

- **"Lollipop" (featuring Static Major)**: Produced by Jim Jonsin, "Lollipop" became Lil Wayne's first No. 1 single on the Billboard Hot 100, where it held the top position for five weeks. Static Major, who co-wrote and sang the hook, died on February 25, 2008 — before the single's release — making his performance a posthumous chart-topper.
- **"A Milli"**: Produced by Bangladesh, the track became one of 2008's most sampled and referenced rap songs; its minimalist, stuttering beat and rapid-fire verse influenced numerous subsequent productions. It peaked at No. 6 on the Hot 100 and won a Grammy for Best Rap Solo Performance.
- **"Mr. Carter" (featuring Jay-Z)**: A baton-passing moment in which Jay-Z — one of hip-hop's most acclaimed artists — effectively endorsed Lil Wayne as a successor figure, lending the album critical credibility.
- **"Dr. Carter" (produced by Kanye West)**: A concept track built around a doctor metaphor for reviving hip-hop, widely regarded as one of Lil Wayne's most lyrically ambitious performances.
- **"Got Money" (featuring T-Pain)**: A radio-friendly, top-15 Hot 100 hit built on T-Pain's signature Auto-Tune sound, which dominated 2008 pop-radio aesthetics.

## Chart Performance and Sales

*Tha Carter III* debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200, selling 1.006 million copies in its first week — making it the first album of any genre to sell over 1 million copies in an opening week since 50 Cent's *The Massacre* in March 2005. By year-end, Nielsen SoundScan ranked it the top-selling album of 2008 across all genres with approximately 2.88 million U.S. copies sold; [Coldplay's *Viva la Vida or Death and All His Friends*](/news/arts-culture/coldplay-viva-la-vida/), the second-best-selling US album of the year at roughly 2.1 million copies, was the nearest competitor. The album was certified multiple times platinum by the RIAA.

## Grammy Recognition

At the 51st Grammy Awards (February 8, 2009, honoring music released in 2008), Lil Wayne won four Grammy Awards, three of them tied to *Tha Carter III*:

- **Best Rap Album** — *Tha Carter III*
- **Best Rap Solo Performance** — "A Milli"
- **Best Rap Song** — "Lollipop"
- **Best Rap Performance by a Duo or Group** — "Swagga Like Us" (with Jay-Z, T.I., and Kanye West), a collaboration from T.I.'s *Paper Trail* rather than *Tha Carter III*

The awards confirmed the album's critical standing beyond its commercial success.

## Significance

**Hip-hop's genre supremacy**: *Tha Carter III*'s status as 2008's best-selling US album across all genres — outselling every rock, pop, and country release — marked a defining moment in hip-hop's consolidation as the dominant popular-music genre in the United States. That shift had been building since the late 1990s, but *Tha Carter III* made hip-hop's commercial primacy unambiguous, standing alongside [*The Dark Knight*](/news/arts-culture/the-dark-knight/) and the [Beijing Olympics opening ceremony](/news/arts-culture/beijing-olympics-opening-ceremony/) among the mass-cultural touchstones of the year.

**The Lil Wayne era**: The album established Lil Wayne in a tier of hip-hop legacy alongside Jay-Z, Eminem, and Nas. His influence on subsequent rap artists — including Drake (whom Wayne mentored) and Nicki Minaj (also signed to Young Money, Wayne's imprint) — was substantial. The album's blend of street credibility with pop-oriented songcraft and melodic hooks anticipated the sound that would dominate mainstream rap through the 2010s.

**Auto-Tune and melodic rap**: Tracks on *Tha Carter III*, particularly the T-Pain feature and Wayne's own sung passages, contributed to 2008's broader saturation of Auto-Tune in popular music. Initially controversial, the technique ultimately redefined how hip-hop and pop vocals could sound, prefiguring artists such as Drake, Future, and Post Malone.

**Recognition of Static Major**: The success of "Lollipop" drew widespread attention to Static Major, whose death at age 33 in February 2008 had gone relatively unnoticed by mainstream media. His prominence on a song that spent five weeks at No. 1 served both as a tribute to his craft and as a broader recognition of the collaborators behind hit records — a year in which the music world also mourned and celebrated figures such as [Amy Winehouse](/news/people/amy-winehouse/).

**Mixtape-to-album conversion**: *Tha Carter III* demonstrated that a mixtape-driven artist could translate online and street-level credibility into massive mainstream album sales — a model that shaped how record labels approached artist development throughout the 2010s and helped set the stage for streaming-era strategies built on constant content output.
